        How the data is made
        1. Sulfur dioxide comes from Sentinel-5P. Cloudy pixels are removed, 7 days are combined into a median, and the readout compares it with the area's own 30-day normal.
        2. Smoke is the Sentinel-5P Absorbing Aerosol Index, 7-day median. Around 0 is clean air, above 1 is a clear layer of smoke or dust, above 2 is a thick plume. The health card uses the 90th percentile over your area, so a plume over part of it still counts.
        3. Active fires are NASA hotspots about 1 km wide: MODIS since 2000 and VIIRS since 2012. “Both” uses VIIRS from 2012 on and MODIS × k before that, so all 25 years are on one scale.
        4. Burn scars compare Sentinel-2 near- and shortwave-infrared light before and after. A big drop (dNBR above 0.27) on land that was green counts as burned.
        5. Reported fires are US fire agency incidents (NIFC), from 2014 on.

        Alert = fire plus sulfur dioxide at 1.5× normal or more. Watch = 10+ fire pixels, or sulfur dioxide at 2× normal. Showing up together doesn't mean one caused the other.

        Smoke levels: clear, fire nearby (fires but little smoke), some smoke (index 1+), heavy smoke (index 2+). Lots of fire nearby bumps the level up a step. Health steps follow EPA AirNow and CDC wildfire smoke advice. This is a satellite estimate, not an air-quality reading or a warning system.

            The story

            Where firescout came from

            Hi, I'm Bhavishaya Vignesh. I used to live in Canada, and I lived through the wildfires in Alberta and British Columbia. The smoke changed the sky and the air we breathed, and it was hard on my family. I wanted to understand what was in that air and how to see it coming, and satellites turned out to see it first. I built firescout because if the data is already up there, it should feel as friendly as opening a map.

            ★
            Tri-Valley Air Quality Award

            I won the Tri-Valley Air Quality Award for this project and the research behind it on Sulfur Scout and wildfires. firescout is where that research became something anyone can open and explore.

            The research behind it

            My research started with a simple question: can satellite data show how wildfires and sulfur dioxide are connected to the air people breathe? I studied sulfur dioxide with Sulfur Scout, studied wildfires through fire hotspots and burn scars, and then put both on one globe so I can compare them for the same place and the same dates.

            What firescout does

            • MODIS and VIIRS fire hotspots combined into one 25-year record
            • A daily burn calendar and a month-by-month history since 2000
            • Sulfur dioxide from Sentinel-5P and burn scars from Sentinel-2
            • Watch Sentinel-5P, Sentinel-2, MODIS and VIIRS pass over any place
            • Save readings and places, and download everything as CSV or images

            Data

            NASA FIRMS (MODIS), NASA VIIRS VNP14A1, Copernicus Sentinel-5P and Sentinel-2, all through Google Earth Engine. US fire incidents from NIFC. Place search by OpenStreetMap Nominatim. Map by CARTO and OpenStreetMap.
