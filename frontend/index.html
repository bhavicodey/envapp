        How the data is made
        1. Smoke is the Sentinel-5P absorbing aerosol index, 7-day median. Around 0 is clean air, above 1 is a clear layer of smoke or dust, above 2 is a thick plume. The health card uses the 90th percentile over your area, so a plume over part of it still counts.
        2. Sulfur dioxide also comes from Sentinel-5P. Cloudy pixels are removed, 7 days are combined into a median, and the readout compares it with the area's own 30-day normal.
        3. Active fires are NASA FIRMS thermal hotspots, about 1 km wide. The readout counts them in your area and groups touching ones into separate fires.
        4. Burn scars compare Sentinel-2 near- and shortwave-infrared light before and now. A big drop (dNBR above 0.27) on land that was green counts as burned.

        Health steps follow EPA AirNow and CDC wildfire smoke advice. This is a satellite estimate, not an air-quality reading or a warning system.

            The story

            Where firescout came from

            Hi, I'm Bhavishaya Vignesh. I used to live in Canada, and I lived through the wildfires in Alberta and British Columbia. The smoke changed the sky and the air we breathed, and it was hard on my family. I wanted to understand what was in that air and how to see it coming, and satellites turned out to see it first. I built firescout because if the data is already up there, it should feel as friendly as opening a map.

            ★
            Tri-Valley Air Quality Award

            I won the Tri-Valley Air Quality Award for this project and the research behind it on Sulfur Scout and wildfires. firescout is where that research became something anyone can open and explore.

            The research behind it

            My research started with a simple question: can satellite data show how wildfires and sulfur dioxide are connected to the air people breathe? I studied sulfur dioxide with Sulfur Scout, studied wildfires through fire hotspots and burn scars, and then put them on one globe so I can compare them for the same place and the same dates.
